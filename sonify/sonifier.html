How it is computed
Two voices, one per probe; voice B sits an interval above voice A. The interval follows the cross-correlation rℓ (interpolated in ln ℓ), ordered by sensory dissonance: . The stereo spread grows as (1−r)/2. Where |r| < 2σr, with σr = (1−r²)/√Nmodes, the correlation is not significant: voice B is replaced by a band-pass noise cue at voice A’s pitch, with B’s level and no stereo spread, so you hear one tone and noise. Between 2σ and 3σ the interval fades in as the noise fades out (equal power at 2.5σ). Switch bands to hear tSZ flip sign at 217 GHz. Common level (default): both voices and the cue play at one constant level, so loudness carries no data and both voices stay audible; Data levels make each voice follow its own band power instead.

About loudness compensation (ISO 226:2003)
The gain applied per audio frequency is the 60-phon equal-loudness contour relative to its value at 1 kHz, capped at +30 dB: it boosts the low ℓ (low pitch), where the ear is less sensitive, and slightly cuts 2–5 kHz. It equalizes perceived loudness only approximately: the contours are averages over listeners, and the result depends on your playback level and device.

ℓ-sweep window

The ℓ-sweep plays the band power in a window that moves across the ℓ range; the shaded curve on the plot is the window as applied.

How it is computed
The ℓ-sweep passes a Dℓ-shaped carrier through a Butterworth band-pass window that moves across [ℓmin, ℓmax]; its level is the band power in the window (one gain path). The shaded curve on the plot is the window as applied.
